AI Agents for Dummies

Zach Zimmerman • July 28, 2026

If you've been keeping up with anything tech-related, or even if you haven't, you might have heard the term "AI Agents" being thrown around.

This may sound like yet another brand-new technology threatening to leave you and your business in the dust, but I promise it's not. I'm going to explain as simply as possible what an AI Agent is, and how you can leverage it to accelerate your own business and keep up with modern software infrastructure.

What is an AI Agent?

Let's answer the obvious question first. What is an AI Agent? Long story short, the name is self-explanatory: it's an agent that's been given a simple mission directive: carry out a specific task in a business workflow. Example tasks include writing an email, summarizing reports, generating reports, etc. Short story long, AI Agents are LLMs that have been provided a seed prompt that is saved in the context window of the LLM for every request. This extra context instructs the LLM how to interpret prompts and approach task planning and resolution.

If that sounds too technical for you, don't worry about it. In fact, forget I said it. All that's important is that you remember 'AI Agents perform small, menial tasks'

By now you're probably thinking "isn't that what interns are for? Why would I even need AI?", and you'd be right! However, the answer to your question isn't "use a more powerful AI that can do more". An AI that can perfectly interpret your intent doesn't exist. Smarter models do exist but are expensive to run and would likely cost you far more than hiring a temp or an intern just to perform some basic administrative tasks. Agentic AI emerged from people realizing this expense and gravitating instead towards agent swarms.

A swarm is a collection of agents meant to perform small tasks that come together (with the help of a little software magic) to complete a complex task. Swarms are far cheaper to run than a singular powerful AI, and often get the job done better since their tasks are limited in scope. To help you understand why that is, think about the following scenario: you're teaching someone to write a report. What's more likely to lead to a satisfactory result on a quicker timeline: walking them through the steps of brainstorming, planning, and writing, or telling them what you need the report to be about and leaving them to their own devices?

Do I Need to Use AI Agents?

Probably. Whatever your business is, there's likely at least one menial task you do multiple times a day that you'd love to be automated. A tedious back-and-forth scheduling dance, mindlessly filling out an expense report, something.

The tricky question isn't answering whether or not incorporating AI into your business would help. It's figuring out how to incorporate it cheaply and seamlessly so that you actually see real benefit rather than lose precious time and money fiddling with yet another technology you don't understand and being left feeling like the whole endeavor wasn't worth it. Time is money, and nobody likes wasting either.

How Do I Use Agents In My Business?

If that were an easy question to answer, I'd be out of work! Effective use of AI requires answering three questions: what daily workflows exist in your business, what individual tasks those workflows are composed of, and which of those tasks lend themselves to automation?
